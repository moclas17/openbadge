# OpenBadge Vision

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft

---

# We believe participation deserves to be remembered.

Communities are built through shared experiences.

A hackathon.
A meetup.
A workshop.
A conference.
A volunteer program.
A community call.

People invest their time, knowledge and energy into these moments.

Those experiences deserve a permanent, verifiable and portable record.

OpenBadge exists to make that possible.

---

# Why OpenBadge Exists

For years, POAP demonstrated that digital participation credentials have real value.

Millions of credentials were issued across thousands of events, proving that communities want a simple way to recognize participation.

The announced sunset of the POAP platform also exposed an important reality.

Communities should never depend on a single company to preserve their history.

Memories should outlive platforms.

OpenBadge exists to ensure they do.

---

# Our Mission

Our mission is straightforward.

> Give every community the ability to issue, manage and preserve digital event credentials using software they fully control.

No subscriptions are required.

No vendor lock-in.

No dependency on a centralized service.

Just software that works.

---

# Our Vision

We envision a future where every event—large or small—can issue digital credentials that are:

- Permanent
- Portable
- Verifiable
- Open
- Community owned

Whether the event hosts twenty people or twenty thousand should not matter.

Every organizer should have access to the same tools.

---

# The Problem

Most digital credential platforms share the same limitations.

The infrastructure belongs to someone else.

The APIs belong to someone else.

The roadmap belongs to someone else.

Communities become users instead of owners.

When the platform changes, communities have no choice but to adapt.

When the platform disappears, communities lose access to the tools they relied on.

OpenBadge changes that relationship.

The community owns the software.

The community owns the infrastructure.

The community owns the data.

---

# Our Principles

Everything built inside OpenBadge should respect these principles.

## Community First

Every decision should benefit event organizers and attendees before benefiting the platform itself.

---

## Open Source by Default

Every line of code should be publicly auditable.

Anyone should be able to inspect, improve or deploy the platform.

---

## Self-Hosted First

Running OpenBadge on your own infrastructure should never be considered a secondary option.

Self-hosting is the default experience.

---

## API First

Everything available through the user interface must also be available programmatically.

Automation should be a first-class citizen.

---

## Simplicity Wins

Every new feature increases complexity.

Version 1 deliberately solves only one problem.

Creating and issuing participation credentials.

Nothing else.

---

## Build for Longevity

Technologies change.

Communities evolve.

Blockchains come and go.

OpenBadge should be designed so that its core concepts remain useful regardless of the underlying technology.

---

# What OpenBadge Is

OpenBadge is:

- An open-source application.
- A self-hosted platform.
- A digital credential issuer.
- A public credential gallery.
- A developer-friendly API.
- A foundation that communities can extend.

---

# What OpenBadge Is Not

OpenBadge is not:

- A DAO.
- A cryptocurrency.
- A social network.
- A wallet.
- An NFT marketplace.
- A blockchain.
- A reputation protocol.

Those ideas may have value.

They simply are not the problem OpenBadge was created to solve.

---

# Design Philosophy

We believe software should disappear behind the experience.

Creating a credential should feel as simple as creating an event.

Claiming a credential should take seconds.

Verifying authenticity should be effortless.

The best software is the software people barely notice.

---

# Long-Term Vision

OpenBadge begins by replacing the essential functionality that communities are losing.

That is only the first step.

As the ecosystem grows, OpenBadge may expand into areas such as:

- Multiple credential types.
- Attendance verification.
- Certificates.
- Organization management.
- Mobile applications.
- Scanner applications.
- Public APIs.
- SDKs.
- Integrations.
- Identity providers.
- Attestation systems.

Growth should always happen organically.

Complexity should never come at the expense of usability.

---

# Decision Framework

Every proposal should answer four questions.

1. Does this make issuing credentials easier?
2. Does this improve the attendee experience?
3. Can this remain self-hosted?
4. Will this still make sense five years from now?

If the answer is "no" to any of these questions, the proposal should be reconsidered.

---

# Version One

Version One has a single objective.

Become the best open-source platform for issuing digital participation credentials.

Not the biggest.

Not the most feature rich.

Simply the best at solving one problem exceptionally well.

---

# Closing

Communities create moments.

Moments become memories.

Memories deserve to last.
